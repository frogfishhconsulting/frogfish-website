Why solar buyers are moving to AI answers

Solar is a long, skeptical purchase with a lot of research behind it. Homeowners compare installers, incentives, financing and payback before they ever take a sales call, and they increasingly do that comparison inside one AI answer. That answer names three to five companies and cites a handful of review sites, guides and local pages. The installers it names get warmer, better-informed leads. The rest end up buying the same homeowner as a shared lead later, at a higher price.

When we tested AI search for solar-specific questions, the answers came from small sites with one page each, not established authorities. The category is still open.

Frequently asked questions

What is AI search optimization for solar companies?

It's the work of getting your company named and cited when homeowners ask AI assistants about going solar, choosing an installer, or financing and incentives in your market. It combines answer-first local content, structured data, reviews and third-party mentions.

How is AEO different from solar SEO?

SEO aims for a ranked link. AEO aims to be named inside the answer itself. They share foundations, but AEO puts more weight on clear, quotable answers, entity consistency and off-site proof like reviews and publications.

Can a regional installer beat national brands in AI answers?

Often, yes, for local questions. AI engines favor specific, current and local information. A strong page for "solar companies in [city]" with real local proof can be cited ahead of a national brand's generic page.

How do you measure results?

By share of AI answers that mention you across a fixed question set, AI referral traffic, and, most importantly, appointments, signed contracts and cost per sale from AI-referred leads in your CRM.

How long does it take?

Local and decision-specific questions typically move within 60–90 days. Broad, competitive questions take 6–12 months of authority building.
